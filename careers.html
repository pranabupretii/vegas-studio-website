Six teams, one catalogue

How a game gets made

Every title passes through all six teams

Take Buffalo Quest, our upcoming sticky-wilds slot. Nobody owns it alone. It moves from desk to desk, and often back again, until it is ready for an operator's lobby.

  1. 1ProductWrites the brief: a buffalo theme, sticky wilds, 20 paylines, medium volatility.
  2. 2Game mathBuilds the model and three RTP configurations: 96.5%, 97% and 98%.
  3. 3Game artDraws symbols, the prairie background and the wild animations.
  4. 4EngineeringBuilds the client, wires up the maths server and tests every configuration.
  5. 5MarketingPrepares the trailer, banners and launch kit operators use in their lobbies.
  6. 6Partner supportSwitches it on for operators and answers their first questions.

Culture

Five things we hold each other to

Not posters on a wall. These are the habits we notice, praise and ask about in interviews.

  1. 01

    Play it before you ship it

    Everyone plays the current builds, whatever their job title. If it is not fun on a phone on the bus, it is not finished.

  2. 02

    Show the maths

    A strong opinion is welcome. A strong opinion with a number behind it wins the argument.

  3. 03

    Small squads, short loops

    Each game has a small squad drawn from every team. Decisions happen in the squad, not in a queue.

  4. 04

    Adults only, safer play built in

    Our games are for adults who choose to play. We design age and safer-play messaging in from the start. Read our responsible gaming approach.

  5. 05

    Say it plainly

    To operators, to players and to each other. Plain words, clear owners, no surprises.

Hiring process

The road from hello to your first day

The same five stops for every team. Timings are typical, not promises.

  1. Stop 1

    Apply

    Through a role on the official careers page, or with an open application if nothing fits yet.

  2. Stop 2

    Intro call

    A 30-minute chat about you, the role and what you want next. Ask us anything.

  3. Stop 3

    Show your craft

    A portfolio walk-through or a short task that matches real work. We keep tasks under three hours.

  4. Stop 4

    Meet the squad

    Talk to the people you would work with day to day, from more than one team.

  5. Stop 5

    Offer and first weeks

    We agree the details, then plan your first weeks together so day one is not a guessing game.

What we look for at every stop

  • Work you can show and explain, including what you would change.
  • Curiosity about the other teams, not only your own craft.
  • Interest in games, even if casino games are new to you.
  • Comfort with feedback, given and received.

Good questions

Careers, answered

Something else? Talk to the studio.

Where are open roles listed?

On the official Vegas Studio careers page at vegas.studio/careers. We do not list vacancies on this page so you never apply for something that has already closed.

Can I send an open application?

Yes. Follow the careers route on our contact page and tell us the team you are interested in and what you would bring.

Do I need casino or iGaming experience?

No. Many good game artists, engineers and marketers come from console, mobile or other industries. You will learn the rules of our market with us. Game math and partner support roles sometimes ask for specific experience; the role description will say.

Will there be a task, and is it fair?

Most roles include a portfolio review or a short task based on real work. We tell you up front how long it should take and what we will look at.

Can I work remotely?

It depends on the team and the role. Each listing on the careers page says how and where the role works.

I applied before. Can I apply again?

Of course. Tell us what has changed since last time; we like seeing how people's work develops.
