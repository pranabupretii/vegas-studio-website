Short-term observed returns can vary widely from theoretical RTP. They do not predict future results or individual winnings.

Two different numbers

Theoretical RTP and observed RTP

Both are written as a percentage, which is why they get mixed up. One comes from the game's design. The other is a record of what happened in a short slice of time.

Set by the maths

Theoretical RTP

The share of all money staked that a game is designed to pay back across a very large number of rounds, usually millions. It is fixed when the game is built and tested. Golden Luck, for example, is published at 95%.

Measured in a window

Observed RTP

What a game actually paid back, divided by what was staked on it, during a chosen period. In this snapshot the period is one hour. A handful of large wins, or none at all, can move it a long way from the theoretical figure.

Total paid out÷Total staked×100=Observed RTP

Worked example, invented numbers: players stake 2,000 credits on a game in an hour and it pays back 2,900. Observed RTP for that hour is 2,900 ÷ 2,000 × 100 = 145%. The next hour it might pay back 600, which reads as 30%. The game's maths has not changed at all.

Reading the extremes

Why a reading can hit 3,611.34% or 0.00%

  1. An hour is a small sample

    A game's theoretical RTP only shows up over a huge number of rounds. One hour on one game may be a few hundred rounds, nowhere near enough for the average to settle.

  2. One big win dominates

    If a feature pays a few hundred times the stake in a quiet hour, that single result can push the hour's figure into the thousands. The same game might read low the hour after.

  3. Zero is an ordinary result

    A 0.00% reading means nothing was paid back in the rounds counted during that hour. With few rounds played, that happens. It does not mean a game is due to pay.

  4. Every round is independent

    Results come from a random number generator. What happened in the last hour has no effect on the next round, so a high or low reading is not a signal to play or to stop.

Glossary

Words worth knowing

Short definitions here, longer ones in the Vegas Studio glossary.

  • RTPReturn to player: the long-run share of stakes a game is designed to pay back.
  • VolatilityHow a game's return is spread: many small wins, or fewer large ones.
  • Hit rateHow often a round returns any win at all.
  • House edgeThe part of stakes a game keeps over the long run: 100% minus RTP.
  • RNGRandom number generator: decides every result, independently of the last.
  • Max winThe largest amount a single round can pay, set by the game's design.
