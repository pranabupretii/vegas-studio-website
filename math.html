Reading notes

The four numbers we always ask about

Return to player

RTP is a long-run average. A 96% game is designed to pay back 96 credits for every 100 staked across millions of spins. It says nothing about the next ten minutes, which is why we never review RTP on its own.

Many of our slots ship with a 95% configuration. Some, like Buffalo Quest, are built with several configurations so an operator can choose the one that fits their market.

RTP

Total paid out divided by total staked, measured over the full cycle of a game.

Volatility

Two games can share the same RTP and feel nothing alike. A low-volatility game spreads its return across many small wins. A very high-volatility game holds most of its return back for rare, large outcomes.

We talk about volatility in bands rather than single figures, because players feel it as a mood more than a number.

Volatility

How widely results swing around the average. Higher volatility means longer gaps and bigger peaks.

Hit rate

Hit rate is the share of spins that return anything at all. It shapes the rhythm of play more than any other number, and it can mislead: a win smaller than the stake still counts as a hit.

We look at hit rate together with the share of wins that actually beat the stake.

Hit rate

Winning spins divided by total spins. Golden Luck lists 40%, so roughly two spins in five pay.

Max win

The ceiling a single round can reach, written as a multiple of the stake. A high ceiling is exciting, but every credit reserved for it is a credit that is not paid out elsewhere, so it has to be earned in the rest of the model.

Max win

The largest payout possible in one round, often capped by design.

Lab notebook

How a concept is reviewed

Every concept that reaches us gets its own page in the notebook. These are the entries it collects on the way through.

  1. Entry 01

    First read

    We read the concept end to end before touching a spreadsheet. What is the hook, who is the player, and is there a reason this game should exist next to everything else in a lobby?

    We note: theme, grid, core mechanic, the moment the player is waiting for.

  2. Entry 02

    Model sketch

    If you sent a model, we check it adds up: that the paytable, reel strips or ball draws can actually deliver the RTP you are targeting. If you did not, we sketch a rough one together.

    We note: RTP split between base game and features, volatility band, max win cap.

  3. Entry 03

    Simulation

    The model runs through a long simulation so the averages settle. We look at the distribution, not just the headline: how often features land, how long the dry spells get, how often the cap is hit.

    We note: measured RTP against target, feature frequency, longest losing streaks.

  4. Entry 04

    Play the numbers

    A plain prototype with placeholder art lets the team feel the rhythm. This is where a model that looks fine on paper sometimes turns out to drag, and where good surprises show up too.

    We note: how the first fifty spins feel, whether the feature is worth the wait.

  5. Entry 05

    Decision

    We come back to you with what we found, what we would change and whether we see a route to market together. If the answer is no, we tell you why.

    We note: recommended changes, open questions, proposed next step.

Submission checklist

Handling your work

IP and confidentiality

Ideas are easy to share and hard to take back, so here is how we suggest going about it.

  • Start with a summary

    Your first message can describe the idea without the full model or paytable. That is usually enough for us to say whether a deeper look makes sense.

  • Agree terms before detail

    If you want a confidentiality agreement in place before sending detailed files, ask. We would rather sort that out first than have you hold back.

  • Label what is yours

    Mark documents with your name and date, and tell us about any third-party art, fonts or licensed themes so nothing is assumed.

  • Similar ideas happen

    We have games in development that you cannot see yet, and themes overlap across the industry. If your concept is close to something already on our slate, we will say so early.

This page is a general guide, not a contract or legal advice. Ownership, rights and commercial terms for any collaboration are agreed in writing with the studio before work begins.

Questions from the bench

Math collaboration FAQ

Something else? Ask the studio.

Do I need a finished model?

No. A clear concept with a target RTP range and volatility band is enough to start. Some of our best conversations began with a single page and a sketch.

Can you build the math for my idea?

Often, yes. Tell us what you have and what is missing. Whether it makes sense depends on the concept and on what else is on our slate at the time.

Do you work on keno as well as slots?

Yes. Our catalogue includes Super Keno, Super Wild Keno and Super Hexa Keno, so pay tables, pick ranges and draw features are familiar ground.

Which RTP should I aim for?

That depends on the markets and operators you have in mind. Many games carry more than one configuration. Give us your range and we will talk through the trade-offs.

Is the explorer on this page accurate?

It is a teaching aid, not a model. It shows the direction numbers move in when you change RTP or volatility. Real games are checked through full simulation.

Who owns the idea?

Ownership and terms are agreed with the studio in writing before any work starts. Until then, share only what you are comfortable sharing.
